One screen, one camera, zero networks

Show animated QR codes on one phone's screen and read them with the other phone's camera. No pairing, no discovery broadcast, no network handshake — nothing to intercept.

Step 1 · Share

Pick a file

Share any file from your gallery, file manager or another app — Briskwire™ shows up as an "Open with…" target. Choose Plain, or Encrypted for exactly one paired receiver.

Step 2 · Send

Screen ➜ Camera

The sender's screen plays the file as a sequence of QR codes at full brightness. Every frame carries its own error-correction and version info, so the receiver can read them in any order.

Step 3 · Receive

Reassemble & verify

The other phone's camera reads the frames — auto-zoom keeps them lined up and a progress grid shows what has arrived. Missed frames are re-requested with a retry code the sender scans, and the finished file is verified end-to-end.

Made for the places phones go but the network doesn't

Briskwire™ was built for people who keep needing to share files exactly where Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and the cellular network aren't. The kinds of files that matter most in those moments — documents, configs, keys, signed PDFs, small photos — not 4K video. Animated QR is designed for small files.

Hikers, climbers & outdoor crews

You're on a ridge, in the canyon, two valleys past LTE. Hand off a GPX track to a hiking partner, a waypoint list to the group, a route plan or rescue PDF to the support van — small files, screen-to-screen, no signal needed.

Field engineers, surveyors & inspectors

Industrial sites, refineries, substations — anywhere mobile data is banned or simply doesn't reach. Pass measurement files, calibration configs, shift-handover notes and inspection checklists phone-to-phone without bringing a single radio into the area.

Privacy-conscious professionals

Journalists, lawyers, security researchers, founders. Move a contract, a signed PDF, a password database, a private key, a leak — knowing Briskwire™ has no INTERNET permission and physically cannot send your bytes anywhere but the receiving phone.

Travellers in airplane mode

On a plane, on a train across a border, in a country whose SIM you don't have. Hand the boarding pass, an itinerary PDF, a small document or contact card to the seat next to you in seconds — no roaming, no shared Wi-Fi, no third-party messenger.

Want colour instead of just black-and-white?

Same payload, two encodings. Briskwire™'s RGB mode (Beta) stacks three independent symbols into the red, green and blue channels of a single frame — roughly 3× the density of a plain QR. Phone screens emit RGB; phone cameras see RGB. Briskwire™ uses all three.

Plain QR · 1 channel

Black & white

One bit per module. The format QR was designed for in 1994 — readable on cardboard by a laser scanner, but using only one of the three channels your screen and camera already speak.

Briskwire™ RGB · 3 channels · Beta

Colour (R + G + B)

Three QRs stacked in the red, green and blue channels of one image — roughly 3× the data per frame, in the same screen real estate. Best at close range with good light; fountain-coded retransmits quietly cover any colour bleed.

How Briskwire™ compares to browser-based QR transfer tools

Several open-source web pages can also stream files as animated QR codes. They're neat — and Briskwire™ is built on a different promise: proof, not trust.

Provably offline — not just "offline-capable"

  • Briskwire™ never asks for the INTERNET permission — Android itself blocks any network access. Verify it in Settings or with any APK inspector.
  • A browser tab always has network access. A web tool asks you to trust that it doesn't use it — Briskwire™ can show you it can't.

A camera that talks back

  • Browser tools stream one-way and hope enough frames land. Briskwire™'s receiver shows a retry code the sender scans — only the missing frames are re-broadcast, so transfers finish deterministically.
  • Native camera control (focus pacing, resolution tier, zoom) self-tunes during the scan — web pages can't touch these knobs.

Encryption the one-way tools can't do

  • The most-requested feature on browser QR streamers is the one they lack: encryption. Briskwire™ seals a transfer end-to-end to a single paired receiver, with a security code on both screens to verify.
  • A one-way web page has no channel to exchange keys over. Briskwire™'s two-camera back-channel makes the pairing possible — entirely offline, no server in the middle.

Built into Android, not a browser tab

  • Briskwire™ is an "Open with…" target for every app on the phone, and each received file lands in a private inbox with Share, Export, Open in… and Delete one tap away.
  • Rotate the phone, switch apps or lose the frame mid-transfer and the scanner picks up where it left off — something a page in a browser tab can't promise.

About Briskwire™

Briskwire™ is the file-transfer app for the moments and places ordinary file-sharing breaks down: a mountain ridge with no signal, a secure facility with Wi-Fi banned, a flight at cruising altitude, a vault with the phone in airplane mode. Most "simple" file-transfer apps quietly phone home, ask for permissions they don't need, or fall apart the second your radios go off. Briskwire™ doesn't — the app cannot reach the internet at all, because it never asks for permission to. No Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, no cellular data, no cloud relay, no account, no telemetry.

Why QR codes?

QR codes are visible, physical, and bounded. There's no pairing to intercept, no discovery broadcast leaking who's around, no network handshake — you can literally see what the other phone is showing, and your phone sees it back. That's the whole trust model. Frames carry their own error-correction so missed or damaged codes recover automatically. This is the app for moving a private key, a signed PDF, a password database, a config bundle, or a photo — not for 4K video.

Encrypted, even over light

Since v2.7.0 a transfer can be sealed for exactly one recipient. The receiver shows a one-time key code, the sender scans it, and from then on the animated frames carry only ciphertext — a bystander filming the screen records noise. Before the file opens, both phones display the same six-digit security code; if someone sat between the two screens, the codes would differ and you'd simply discard. Fresh keys are generated for every transfer, exist only on the two devices, and are gone when it ends. Plain mode stays one tap away for broadcasting to several receivers at once.

Privacy on first launch

The first time you open Briskwire™, it shows its privacy policy and asks you to read and accept it before using the app. The policy is bundled with the app — nothing is fetched online. Decline and the app closes cleanly. See the privacy policy for the full text.

Frequently asked questions

Short answers about offline file transfer, encryption, file sizes, supported barcodes and privacy.

Does Briskwire™ need Wi-Fi, Bluetooth or internet to transfer files?

No. Files move over animated QR codes shown on one phone's screen and read by the other phone's camera. The app does not declare the Android INTERNET permission, so it cannot go online — it works with every radio off, including in airplane mode.

Do both phones need Briskwire™ installed?

Yes. The sending phone displays the QR sequence and the receiving phone runs Briskwire™ to reassemble and verify the file. Ordinary barcodes and single QR codes can be scanned with the built-in Detect scanner without a second device.

How large a file can I send?

Animated QR transfer is designed for small files — documents, keys, configs, notes and small photos — not video. Files are compressed automatically before sending, seven speed presets let you trade range for throughput, and interrupted transfers can be resumed.

Are transfers encrypted?

Optionally, yes. Every send asks whether to go Plain or Encrypted. In Encrypted mode the receiver shows a one-time key code, the sender scans it, and the frames then carry only ciphertext. Both phones display the same six-digit security code before the file opens; if the codes differ, discard the transfer. Keys are generated fresh for each transfer and never leave the two devices.

Which barcode formats can Briskwire™ scan?

QR, Aztec, Codabar, Code 39, Code 93, Code 128, Data Matrix, EAN-8, EAN-13, ITF, PDF417, UPC-A and UPC-E, plus the Briskwire™ RGB colour QR. Scanned URLs, phone numbers, emails, SMS, geo coordinates, contacts, calendar events and Wi-Fi networks hand off to the matching Android app with one tap.

Does Briskwire™ collect any data?

No. There are no analytics, crash reports, ads, trackers or accounts, and nothing leaves your phone. Received files and scan sessions are stored only in the app's private storage on your device. See the privacy policy for the full details.

Which Android version do I need, and is there an iPhone version?

Briskwire™ runs on Android 11 and newer and is sold on Google Play as a one-time purchase. There is no iOS version.

What languages does the app support?

English, Polish, Ukrainian, Spanish and German — every screen is translated, and you can switch language and theme from Settings.
