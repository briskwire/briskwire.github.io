Two ways to transfer, zero networks involved

Every send lets you choose: show QR codes on one phone's screen and read them with the other's camera — or hold the two phones back-to-back.

Mode 1 · QR Codes

Screen ➜ Camera

One phone displays QR codes on its screen; the other reads them with its camera. Each frame carries its own error-correction and version info, so missed or damaged codes are re-read automatically. Auto-zoom and full brightness keep everything readable.

Mode 2 · NFC Tap

Back ➜ Back

Hold the two phones back-to-back and the file transfers directly. Range is just a few centimetres, so nothing leaks further than the devices touching. No pairing, no setup, no discovery.

Made for the places phones go but the network doesn't

Briskwire was built for people who keep needing to share files exactly where Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and the cellular network aren't. The kinds of files that matter most in those moments — documents, configs, keys, signed PDFs, small photos — not 4K video. QR is designed for small files; NFC stretches that to small-to-medium.

Hikers, climbers & outdoor crews

You're on a ridge, in the canyon, two valleys past LTE. Hand off a GPX track to a hiking partner, a waypoint list to the group, a route plan or rescue PDF to the support van — small files, screen-to-screen, no signal needed.

Field engineers, surveyors & inspectors

Industrial sites, refineries, substations — anywhere mobile data is banned or simply doesn't reach. Pass measurement files, calibration configs, shift-handover notes and inspection checklists phone-to-phone without bringing a single radio into the area. For larger inspection photos, use NFC tap.

Privacy-conscious professionals

Journalists, lawyers, security researchers, founders. Move a contract, a signed PDF, a password database, a private key, a leak — knowing Briskwire has no INTERNET permission and physically cannot send your bytes anywhere but the receiving phone.

Travellers in airplane mode

On a plane, on a train across a border, in a country whose SIM you don't have. Hand the boarding pass, an itinerary PDF, a small document or contact card to the seat next to you in seconds — no roaming, no shared Wi-Fi, no third-party messenger.

Smart action handoffs

Detected codes don't just decode — Briskwire reads the type and hands off to the right system app via Android Intents. One tap and the right thing happens:

  • URL → opens in your default browser
  • Phone number → opens the dialer pre-filled
  • Email (mailto) → opens your mail app with To / Subject / Body filled in
  • SMS → opens messaging with the number and body ready
  • Geo coordinates → opens in Maps
  • vCard / MeCard contact → "Add to contacts" with all fields parsed
  • VEVENT / VCALENDAR → "Add to calendar" with title, time, location, notes
  • Wi-Fi network → "Connect" pre-fills SSID + password into system settings
  • Every action stays on-device — no cloud lookups, no third-party APIs called

Want colour instead of just black-and-white?

Same payload, two encodings. Briskwire's RGB mode (Beta) stacks three independent symbols into the red, green and blue channels of a single frame — roughly 3× the density of a plain QR. Phone screens emit RGB; phone cameras see RGB. Briskwire uses all three.

Plain QR · 1 channel

Black & white

One bit per module. The format QR was designed for in 1994 — readable on cardboard by a laser scanner, but using only one of the three channels your screen and camera already speak.

Briskwire RGB · 3 channels · Beta

Colour (R + G + B)

Three QRs stacked in the red, green and blue channels of one image — roughly 3× the data per frame, in the same screen real estate. Best at close range with good light; fountain-coded retransmits quietly cover any colour bleed.

About Briskwire™

Briskwire is the file-transfer app for the moments and places ordinary file-sharing breaks down: a mountain ridge with no signal, a secure facility with Wi-Fi banned, a flight at cruising altitude, a vault with the phone in airplane mode. Most "simple" file-transfer apps quietly phone home, ask for permissions they don't need, or fall apart the second your radios go off. Briskwire doesn't — the app cannot reach the internet at all, because it never asks for permission to. No Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth, no cellular data, no cloud relay, no account, no telemetry.

Why QR codes?

QR codes are visible, physical, and bounded. There's no pairing to intercept, no discovery broadcast leaking who's around, no network handshake — you can literally see what the other phone is showing, and your phone sees it back. That's the whole trust model. Frames carry their own error-correction so missed or damaged codes recover automatically. This is the app for moving a private key, a signed PDF, a password database, a config bundle, or a photo — not for 4K video.

Privacy on first launch

The first time you open Briskwire, it shows its privacy policy and asks you to read and accept it before using the app. The policy is bundled with the app — nothing is fetched online. Decline and the app closes cleanly. See the privacy policy for the full text.
